What the papers actually did

Every bioinformatics chain below was pulled automatically out of a real preprint's Methods section — no hand-curation. The question this repo exists to answer is not which tools exist, but how people compose them: where the field has quietly converged on one answer, and where it hasn't.

The chains

One row per paper, one block per step, left to right in the order the Methods describes. Colour is the stage of the analysis and the two-letter code repeats it, so the sequence stays readable without relying on colour. Hover or tap any block for the role, tool and parameters.

Where the field agrees

For each role used three or more times, the bar is the share going to that role's single most common tool — 100% means every paper reached for the same thing. The chip records what step_roles.yaml predicted before we saw any of this data: whether tools in that slot are freely swappable, or whether the choice is a live methodological dispute.

Drift over time

Are people switching packages, and are they keeping versions current? Both need enough observations per time period, which is a much harder bar than the totals above — a role can be well sampled overall and still have two uses per year. Roles below the threshold are listed rather than plotted.

Version reporting

The share of steps where the paper states a version at all — a reproducibility signal independent of which tool was chosen, and measured per step rather than per paper, so it has more to work with.

What the ontology still misses

Read this with the caveats

  • These are early extractions, kept to show the shape of the output. Nothing here is a claim about the field — the consensus numbers are illustrations of the measurement, not results.
  • Nothing is verified. Every chain is single-pass model output with no second reading and no human check. None of it has been written to the catalog.
  • This is only the claimed side. The point of the project is the gap between what a paper says and what its code does. The code-reading extractor doesn't exist yet, so there is no divergence to show.
  • Availability skews the sample. 20 preprints were searched; 11 had no retrievable full text and one produced no chain. Whatever biases that filter carries are inherited here.
